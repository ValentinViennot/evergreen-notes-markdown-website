
[[ATLAS structure]]


Where to get started?
1. Is it a space with already a lot of open source, or mostly close source
2. Is there consumer money to pay for funding the open source alternatives


Batch theme ideas
1. **Privacy-focused consumer software**: This space currently has a mix of open and closed-source solutions, with consumers increasingly willing to invest in privacy. Open-source alternatives can capitalise on this trend, offering transparency that closed-source options lack.
2. **Open source game development tools**: The gaming industry is largely dominated by proprietary tools and engines. There's a growing interest in open-source tools that could empower indie developers, which consumers might support through crowdfunding or direct investment.
3. **Social media**: There's a growing demand for privacy-respecting, open-source social platforms amidst concerns over data misuse by major closed-source platforms. Open-source social media can attract both consumer and government interest due to its transparency and potential for community-driven governance.
4. **Productivity tools**: With remote work becoming more prevalent, there's an increased need for reliable productivity tools. Governments and consumers alike are interested in alternatives to mainstream options, particularly for reasons of data sovereignty and security.


Specific for government
1. **Secure Communication Tools**: Beyond Tchap, there are opportunities to develop open-source video conferencing tools, secure file sharing, and collaborative platforms tailored to government security standards.
2. **Public Administration Management Systems**: Systems that can manage internal workflows, public inquiries, and administrative processes efficiently, all within an open-source framework.
3. **Data Analytics for Public Services**: Tools that can help governments analyse large amounts of data for better policy-making, while ensuring compliance with data protection laws like GDPR.
4. **Infrastructure and Network Security**: Given the emphasis on digital sovereignty, there's a need for open-source network security tools that governments can audit and rely on for critical infrastructure.
