The **Decentralized Identifier (DID)**, as specified by the W3C, is a globally unique identifier designed to create secure, verifiable digital identities without relying on centralized authorities. Unlike traditional identifiers (e.g., email or usernames), DIDs allow individuals or entities to generate and control their own identities using cryptographic proofs, such as digital signatures.

A DID resolves to a **[[DID document]]**, which contains public keys and service endpoints that enable secure interactions with the subject associated with that DID. This subject could be a person, organization, device, or even a concept. DIDs are crucial in building **self-sovereign identities** where the user—not a platform—maintains control over their identity and data. Multiple [[DID methods]] exist in order to [[Resolving a DID]].

The W3C DID specification emphasizes privacy, persistence, and decentralization. DIDs can be used across different systems, from blockchain-based applications to decentralized web services. They form the backbone of trustless systems by allowing cryptographic verification of identity without needing a centralized registry.

This flexibility makes DIDs ideal for applications requiring strong identity proof, such as in secure messaging, digital identity systems, or decentralized apps (dApps), enabling a broader adoption of decentralized web technologies.
