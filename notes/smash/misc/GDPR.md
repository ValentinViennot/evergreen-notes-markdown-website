The **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** is a comprehensive privacy law enacted by the European Union in 2018 to protect the personal data of individuals within the EU. Its primary aim is to give people control over how their data is collected, processed, and shared by organizations, both inside and outside the EU, if those organizations handle EU citizens' data.

1. **Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency**: Organizations must handle personal data lawfully and transparently. Data collection should be done with clear consent or a legal basis, ensuring individuals understand how their data is used.
2. **Purpose Limitation**: Data must be collected for specified, legitimate purposes and cannot be used for anything beyond that.
3. **Data Minimization**: Only the minimum amount of data necessary for the task should be collected.
4. **Accuracy**: Personal data must be kept accurate and up to date.
5. **Storage Limitation**: Personal data should only be stored for as long as it is needed and no longer.
6. **Integrity and Confidentiality**: Data must be processed securely, with protections against breaches or misuse, such as encryption.
7. **Accountability**: Organizations must be able to demonstrate compliance with GDPR by keeping records and making data protection an integral part of their operations.

GDPR gives individuals several key rights over their data, including:
- **Right to Access**: Individuals can request access to the data an organization holds about them.
- **Right to Rectification**: They can have incorrect data corrected.
- **Right to Erasure**: Also known as the "right to be forgotten," individuals can request that their data be deleted.
- **Right to Data Portability**: They can transfer their data to another service provider.
- **Right to Object**: They can object to the processing of their data in certain circumstances, such as for marketing.

Requirements to organizations:
- **Consent**: Organizations must get explicit, unambiguous consent from individuals before processing their data unless there’s another legal basis.
- **Data Breaches**: In the event of a data breach, organizations must notify the relevant authorities within 72 hours and inform affected individuals if there is a high risk to their rights and freedoms.
- **Data Protection by Design and Default**: Privacy must be a key consideration in any product or service development, ensuring compliance from the outset.
- **Appointment of a Data Protection Officer (DPO)**: Some organizations must appoint a DPO to oversee GDPR compliance, especially those handling large amounts of sensitive data.

GDPR also regulates **international data transfers** to ensure that personal data remains protected when it leaves the EU. Transfers can only happen to countries with adequate privacy protections or if specific safeguards, like standard contractual clauses, are in place.

For businesses, GDPR compliance is an ongoing process. Regular **audits, staff training**, and updates to privacy policies are essential to ensure adherence to these regulations and avoid severe fines, which can be up to 4% of a company's global annual revenue.
