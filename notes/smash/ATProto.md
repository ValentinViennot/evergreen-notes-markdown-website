The AT Protocol (Authenticated Transfer Protocol, or ATProto) is a decentralized, federated protocol designed to build social applications at scale. It enables users to control their identity and data across different platforms, promoting interoperability and decentralization. Instead of being locked into a single platform, users can move freely between services while retaining ownership of their content and social graph.

Key features of AT Protocol include:

- **Account Portability**: Users can migrate their account, data, and social connections between different providers without losing access, offering resilience against bans or service shutdowns.
- **Federation**: Similar to how email works, multiple servers communicate with each other, ensuring a decentralized network without a single point of control.
- **Data Repositories**: User data is stored in signed repositories (similar to Git for social media content), ensuring data integrity and security.
- **Algorithmic Choice**: Users can select or customize their own content feeds, giving them control over the algorithms that shape their experience.
- **Lexicon Schema Framework**: A global network of schemas (Lexicon) standardizes data exchange between different platforms, enhancing interoperability.

AT Protocol aims to promote open, decentralized public conversations, allowing developers to create new social applications, bots, and custom features while offering freedom from platform monopolies.

This protocol powers the **[[Bluesky]]** project, which is a microblogging client built on top of the AT Protocol.
