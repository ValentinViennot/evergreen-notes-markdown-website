The **PreKey (PK)** is used primarily in the **[[Signal Protocol]]** to enable asynchronous and secure session establishment, particularly when one party is offline. It is also used by [[Messaging Endpoints]] in order to authenticate a [[Peer]].

In the **[[Signal Protocol]]**:
- A **PreKey (PK)** is a **short to mid-term** **asymmetric key pair** that a user generates and publishes in advance. When another user wants to start a conversation, they use the recipient’s PreKey to establish the initial secure session. 
- The **PK** allows users to send encrypted messages even if the recipient is offline. Once the recipient comes online, they can use their private keys to decrypt the message and establish a secure session.
- Multiple PreKeys can be generated, some of which are **[[One-Time PreKey (OTPK)]]**, used only once for added security, ensuring that each session is unique and that there’s no key reuse.

In relation to **[[Smash Messaging Endpoint (SMEv1)]]s** (Smash [[Messaging Endpoints]]):
- The **PreKey (PK)** is used to identify and authenticate the Peer with its configured SMEs.
- A new key pair is generated by the Peer for each connection to a Messaging Endpoint.
- The public key part is published as part of the **Smash [[Peer Identity]]**. In the **[[DID Document]]**, the **PreKey (PK)** is listed under the service section, specifically tied to **Smash Messaging Endpoints (SMEs)**. The PreKey is signed by the **Identity Key (IK)** to ensure authenticity and integrity.
